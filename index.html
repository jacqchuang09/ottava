    Table-integrated soft tissue robotics

    A surgical robot that lives inside the table.

    Johnson & Johnson MedTech's OTTAVA builds four robotic arms right into the operating table.

    01 · Clinical need

    Robots made surgery precise. They also filled the room.

    Carts and booms need floor space, a docking step, and a plan every time the table moves.

    Space

    Many ORs were never sized for a robot.

    Docking

    Carts roll in, line up and dock before every case.

    Repositioning

    Tilting the table can mean undocking.

    34% less OR space than da Vinci 5per J&J

    02 · System overview

    Four arms, one table.

    No cart, no boom. The arms stow under the table and unfold into setup and procedure poses.

    Static view: four arms mounted on the table rails, docked over the patient. The interactive 3D model needs WebGL.

    Arm pose

    Twin motion

    • Arms. Four, 9 joints each.
    • Console. Open, surgeon sits upright.
    • Vision. One arm holds the camera.

    03 · Kinematics

    Pivot at the port, move everything else.

    Drag the tip. The instrument always pivots at the incision.

    0 cmInsertion depth
    0°Pivot angle
    0.0 mmPort movement
    1

    Remote center of motionThe port acts like a fixed pivot. Tip goes right, outer end goes left, and the incision never stretches.

    2

    Software holds the pivotEvery joint is motorized, so the controller solves for angles that keep the instrument through the same point.

    3

    Spare joints avoid collisionsSlide arm shape: the tip stays put while the arm changes posture. J&J says the 9 joints help minimize collisions.

    04 · Surgeon console

    Big hand motions in, tiny steady ones out.

    Scale
    Hand
    Upright, open postureJ&J describes an immersive viewer that still keeps the rest of the OR in sight.
    Finger gripsPinching the grips closes the jaws, as on robotic consoles in general.
    ClutchLike lifting a mouse off the pad: hands re-center while the instruments hold still.
    Scaling and filteringBig hand moves become small steady ones. OTTAVA's settings aren't public, so this is illustrative.

    10 · Regulatory

    A new kind of device needs a new path.

    1. 1Usual route: 510(k)

      Show FDA you match a robot that's already on the market.

    2. ✕No match existed

      No robot had ever been built into the table.

    3. 2De Novo instead

      FDA reviews it as something new. Backed by the FORTE study and bench testing.

    4. ✓Granted July 21, 2026

      A new Class II category. Future robots like it can now point to OTTAVA.

    What FDA says every robot of this kind must prove

    Human factors

    • Staff can reach the patient, in normal use and emergencies
    • Teams are trained on setup, shutdown and emergencies
    • The table and arms work safely as one system

    Motion

    • Arms move accurately and repeatably
    • Table motion stays under control
    • Ports aren't injured when the table tilts

    Basics

    • Software, electrical and EMC safety
    • Cleaning, reprocessing and sterility
    • Enough workspace for the procedure
